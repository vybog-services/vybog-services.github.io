Specializations

Four tracks our Product Management bench is deep in.

Generalist Product Management is easy to find. These are the practices that separate a senior hire from a mid one.

Roadmap discipline

Now-Next-Later, not Gantt-as-fantasy. Roadmaps that survive a quarter, with kill criteria written in. Outcomes over outputs.

Discovery, sprint-shaped

Hypotheses logged, killed, or validated in two-week cycles. Quant + qual together. Comfortable saying "do not build" when the data says to.

Metrics fluency

Reads activation funnels, retention curves, cohort tables. Reads SQL, runs Mixpanel / Amplitude queries themselves. Numerate decisions.

Engineering partnership

Reads code reviews. Understands tradeoffs. Says "we will not ship that this sprint" when the architecture cost is real.

De-Risk the Hire

The two-week discovery sprint, before commitment.

If you are bringing in a Product Management product manager for a long engagement, the worst time to find out the fit is wrong is week six. Here is how we shrink that risk.

The sprint is a fixed two-week, fixed-fee engagement where one of our senior Product Management product managers reads your codebase, joins your standups, and ships a small but real PRD or experiment. At the end of the two weeks, both sides know enough to commit — or to walk away cleanly.

We bill the discovery sprint at a flat rate and credit it against the first month of any continuing engagement. If the fit is wrong, you keep the PRD or experiment, the documentation, and the architectural notes the expert wrote up. Nobody loses the quarter.

Start a discovery sprint →

What the sprint covers

  • Days 1–3 · Audit roadmap, backlog, metrics; stakeholder interviews
  • Days 4–6 · First PRD written, one experiment scoped
  • Days 7–10 · First experiment launched or feature shipped
  • Days 11–14 · Roadmap revision, next-quarter recommendations
  • End of sprint · Fit decision, transparent on both sides
Sprint Output

What a Vybog Services Product Management product manager ships, by week.

Realistic week-by-week output for a senior Product Management product manager on a typical product team.

Week 1

Foundation

  • Roadmap + backlog audit complete
  • First PRD written
  • One observation memo on metrics + funnels
  • Joined product team rituals
Week 2

First brief

Week 4

Sustained throughput

  • Two experiments active, results read
  • Roadmap revised with kill criteria
  • Discovery → ship → measure loop running

Product Management FAQ

Three questions we get on every Product Management engagement.

Will your PMs run user research themselves?
Yes. Every senior PM on the bench has run discovery interviews, synthesized findings, and turned them into PRDs. If a project warrants a dedicated researcher, we will tell you.
Do your PMs write SQL?
Yes. Comfortable in Amplitude / Mixpanel and in BigQuery / Postgres for ad-hoc analysis. Not a substitute for an analytics engineer, but enough to not block on one.
Roadmap, sprint planning, both?
Both. Roadmap as the strategic artifact, sprint planning as the operational rhythm. We will write the format that fits your team, not impose ours.
