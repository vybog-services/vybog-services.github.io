Specializations

Four tracks our Brand Design bench is deep in.

Generalist Brand Design is easy to find. These are the practices that separate a senior hire from a mid one.

Identity systems

Logo with variants, lockups, partnership marks. Color systems with on-brand contrast for product accessibility. Type pairings that work in product and marketing.

Brand books, tokenized

Not a 60-page PDF. Brand tokens that map to Figma variables and CSS variables. Voice, tone, do/don’t with examples your team can copy.

Motion + animation

Lottie animations for product, After Effects for marketing, scroll-driven web motion. Motion specs that engineers can ship, not just an MP4.

Cross-surface consistency

Brand that holds up from favicon to billboard. Product surfaces, marketing site, social, events — one system, multiple surfaces.

The Senior Bar

What 'senior' actually means in our Brand Design bench.

The five things every senior Brand Design designer on our bench can be measured against. Not aspirational — these are the hiring rubric.

01

Conceptual strength

Can defend a logo direction with strategy, not just craft. Reads brief, asks the right questions, kills weak directions early.

02

Systems thinking

Identity built to scale across surfaces. Tokens, variants, dark/light, accessibility considered from concept.

03

Production craft

Vector hygiene, file organization, export-ready assets. No surprises in handoff.

04

Brand book discipline

Voice, tone, color, type, motion — documented for the team that will use it, not the team that wrote it.

05

Motion fluency

Lottie + After Effects, motion specs that map to web and product. Knows when to animate and when not to.

De-Risk the Hire

The two-week discovery sprint, before commitment.

If you are bringing in a Brand Design designer for a long engagement, the worst time to find out the fit is wrong is week six. Here is how we shrink that risk.

The sprint is a fixed two-week, fixed-fee engagement where one of our senior Brand Design designers reads your codebase, joins your standups, and ships a small but real identity surface. At the end of the two weeks, both sides know enough to commit — or to walk away cleanly.

We bill the discovery sprint at a flat rate and credit it against the first month of any continuing engagement. If the fit is wrong, you keep the identity surface, the documentation, and the architectural notes the expert wrote up. Nobody loses the quarter.

What the sprint covers

  • Days 1–3 · Brand audit, stakeholder interviews, written observations
  • Days 4–6 · Three directions, narrowed to one
  • Days 7–10 · Logo system + first applications shipped
  • Days 11–14 · Brand book v0, motion specs, next-quarter recommendations
  • End of sprint · Fit decision, transparent on both sides

Brand Design FAQ

Three questions we get on every Brand Design engagement.

Do you do logo-only projects?
Yes, but we usually scope at least logo + color + type + first applications. A logo without a system is harder to use than no logo at all.
How long for a full brand identity?
4–8 weeks for most projects. The compressed end requires fast stakeholder cycles and one decision-maker. We will scope honestly in week one.
Will the brand book be a Figma file or a PDF?
Both. Figma file as the live source of truth, Notion for narrative content, PDF for stakeholders who still want one. Tokens map to CSS variables.
