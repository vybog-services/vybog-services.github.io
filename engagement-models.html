Three Models

Project, Retainer, or Pod.

The shape that fits the work. Most engagements start in one model and move into another as the relationship deepens. Switching is friction-free; we explain the tradeoffs in writing.

Project · Fixed scope, fixed fee

A defined deliverable, milestone-billed, with a written scope memo signed by both sides. Best for greenfield builds, migrations, and bounded modernization work.

  • Discovery sprint (1–2 weeks, flat fee)
  • Milestone-based billing tied to demo acceptance
  • Written change-order discipline; no hourly surprises
Most common

Retainer · Monthly capacity

A reserved block of senior hours per month, used flexibly across features, fixes, and growth work. The default model for clients moving from a project to long-term operation.

  • Monthly hour bucket, rolls forward up to 25%
  • Bundled improvements, no per-ticket quoting
  • 30-day notice to pause, change, or close

Pod · Embedded team

A full pod (3–7 people) embedded in your repo, your standup, your release rituals. Tech lead, engineers, QA, and a delivery lead, all named, all senior.

  • 3–7 named engineers + tech lead + QA
  • Joins your standups, your Slack, your sprint rituals
  • Monthly retainer; 30-day scale-up or scale-down
In Every Model

Six things that do not change with the contract shape.

Project, retainer, or pod — these six rituals stay constant. The contract changes; the delivery discipline does not.

Senior-only delivery

The expert pitching is the expert delivering. No farm-out to a junior pool. No surprise hand-off to a third tier two months in.

Two-week sprint rhythm

Planning Monday, demo Thursday, retro Friday. Same cadence whether you booked a 4-week migration or a 12-month platform build.

Weekly demo, recorded

Every Thursday a working build is demoed live. Recording up by Friday morning, indexed by feature card.

Telemetry on day one

Traces, metrics, structured logs, dashboards — wired before the first feature ships. Owned, reviewed monthly.

Security from kickoff

Secrets in a vault, least-privilege IAM, threat model before launch. Not a checklist at the end — designed in from day one.

Portable on day one

Repo access, runbooks, docs, telemetry credentials — yours from week one. No code lock-in, no hostage knowledge.
