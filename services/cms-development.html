CMS Development

A CMS your editors will actually publish to.

Vybog builds WordPress, Drupal, Joomla, WooCommerce, Shopify, and headless CMS implementations the same way we build software — with content modeling discipline, editor-first workflows, and the security hardening that most agencies treat as out-of-scope.

Platforms we build on

Six CMS platforms, each with a sharp opinion on when to pick it.

WordPress

Ideal for: content-heavy marketing sites, publishers, founder-led brands. Where the editor workflow matters more than the rendering pipeline.

Drupal

Ideal for: government, universities, healthcare. Complex content modeling, multi-site setups, granular role permissions, regulated industries.

Joomla

Ideal for: association sites, member portals, community platforms. Where built-in user management and access control are the core requirements.

WooCommerce

Ideal for: WordPress-native commerce, brands under five thousand orders a month, content-and-commerce mixed stores. Flexible, opinionated, plugin-rich.

Shopify

Ideal for: DTC brands at scale, hands-off operations, headless Hydrogen storefronts. Where you want the platform handling tax, fraud, and PCI.

Wix / Webflow

Ideal for: brand sites under 30 pages, design-led marketing teams. Where the marketing team needs to ship without a sprint planning meeting.

Editor experience charter

Five rules every Vybog CMS build follows.

The principles we will not skip — even when the timeline is tight, even when the budget shrinks.

1

Block-based composition

Editors assemble pages from a fixed set of well-tested blocks. No page-builder soup, no "everyone has admin access" anarchy.

2

Preview parity

Draft preview matches the live render exactly. No "looks fine in the editor, breaks live" surprises.

3

Content modeling

Custom post types, taxonomies, and relationships modeled before a theme is touched. Schema-first, theme-second.

4

Role-based publishing

Writers draft, editors approve, marketers schedule. A workflow that mirrors how the team actually operates.

5

Scheduled releases

Coordinated content drops, future-dated publishing, embargo handling. Press releases go live at 9 AM ET, not whenever the editor remembers.

Headless vs. traditional

The architecture choice that defines the next three years.

Traditional CMS (WP, Drupal, Joomla) Headless (Sanity, Contentful, Strapi + Next.js)
Time to launchFaster — themes, plugins, page buildersSlower upfront — but pays back at month six
Performance ceilingGood with caching layersExcellent — static generation, edge rendering
Multi-channel reuseLimited — site-bound contentNative — same content powers web, mobile, kiosks
Editor experienceMature, opinionated, plugin-richModern, sometimes raw — depends on the CMS choice
Hosting complexityOne stack — managed WP / Drupal hosts availableTwo stacks — CMS plus frontend host
Best forMarketing sites with one channel, small editorial teamsBrands serving multiple surfaces, dev-led organizations

Performance & security hardening

WordPress is only "slow and insecure" if you treat it like a toy.

The hardening checklist that runs on every Vybog WordPress deployment.

Caching tiers

Object cache on Redis, page cache via Varnish or a managed equivalent, CDN at the edge with intelligent invalidation. TTFB under 200ms at p95 globally.

WAF & brute-force protection

Cloudflare or AWS WAF in front, rate-limited login, two-factor enforced for editors and above, IP allowlist for /wp-admin where the audience allows.

Plugin audit cadence

Quarterly review of every installed plugin: maintenance status, vulnerability history, alternative options. We retire abandoned plugins before they retire your site.

Backup & restore drills

Daily off-site backups with monthly restore verification — the part most agencies skip until the restore actually matters.

Multi-site & multilingual

One brand, fourteen markets, one editorial team.

When the same brand publishes in multiple languages or runs sub-brands per region, the CMS architecture has to follow. We have shipped multi-site setups serving thirty-plus locales from a single editorial backend.

  • WordPress Multisite (WPMU) — sub-brand networks, franchise sites, regional storefronts sharing themes and plugins.
  • ACF Pro — content modeling that survives editor turnover, with field groups versioned in code.
  • WPML and Polylang — translation workflows wired to professional translation services, with QA on staging.
  • Drupal multi-site — shared codebase, per-site database, per-site editorial teams.
  • Locale-aware routing & hreflang — done correctly so Google ranks each locale, not just the canonical one.

Migration & replatforming

From Wix to WordPress. From Drupal to headless. Without losing rankings.

Most CMS migrations lose 20 – 40% of organic traffic in the first month because nobody plans the redirect map until it is too late. Vybog leads with the migration plan.

Wix or Squarespace → WordPress

Content extracted via API or scraping, re-modeled into custom post types, redirect map built from analytics top-pages first. Typical timeline: 6 – 10 weeks.

Drupal → headless (Sanity + Next.js)

Branch-by-abstraction migration with both stacks live on subpaths during transition. Cut-over with zero downtime, SEO preserved.

Legacy WordPress → modern WordPress

PHP 7.4 → 8.2, jQuery themes → block themes, classic editor → Gutenberg. Done as a side-by-side migration, not a destructive upgrade.
