Three build tracks

Marketing site, web app, or portal — three different problems.

"Build us a website" usually means one of these three things. The shape of the engagement changes accordingly.

Marketing sites

Built to rank, built to convert, built to be edited weekly by a marketer who has never opened your codebase.

  • Static or hybrid rendering, CDN-first
  • CMS-backed with versioned content
  • Conversion instrumentation from day one
Most common

Web applications

Authenticated product experiences with state, real-time data, and dashboards. The thing you charge a subscription for.

  • Component design system from sprint one
  • State management that survives feature growth
  • Telemetry baked into every user journey

Customer portals

The post-sale interface where customers self-serve. Lower-traffic, higher-stakes, integrated with billing, CRM, and helpdesk.

  • SSO and role-based access
  • Deep CRM and billing integration
  • Audit logging and entitlement enforcement

Accessibility & ADA compliance

WCAG 2.2 AA is the floor, not the ceiling.

Accessibility is not a separate workstream we add at the end. It is in our component library, our PR template, and our QA rubric. Every Vybog Services web build ships with an accessibility audit report attached.

  • Semantic HTML and ARIA only where it earns its complexity
  • Keyboard navigation tested on every interactive element — no mouse-only flows
  • Color contrast ratios checked at design-token level, not pixel by pixel
  • Focus management on modals, drawers, and route transitions
  • Screen-reader passes with VoiceOver and NVDA before launch
  • Lighthouse and axe-core wired into CI; PRs blocked on regressions
  • Reduced-motion preferences respected across animations
  • Alt-text policies that designers and writers both follow

CMS-driven vs. custom-coded

One decision that shapes the next five years.

The choice that most determines whether your marketing team will publish weekly or file a ticket every time.

CMS-driven (WordPress, Webflow, Sanity) Custom-coded (Next.js, Nuxt, Astro)
Editor experienceWYSIWYG, no developer needed for content editsRequires content modeling discipline; usually still uses a headless CMS
Time to first launch4–8 weeks for a 12-page site8–14 weeks for the equivalent
Performance ceilingGood with caching; Lighthouse 90s possibleExcellent; Lighthouse 95+ standard
Design flexibilityConstrained by themes, plugins, page buildersPixel-level control; can match any brand system
Ongoing costHigher (plugins, hosting, maintenance)Lower runtime cost; higher developer involvement for changes
Best forContent-heavy sites, multi-author teams, weekly publishingBrand-led marketing sites, product UIs, conversion-critical pages

Platforms we build on

Eight CMS platforms, each with a sharp opinion on when to pick it.

WordPress

Content-heavy marketing sites, publishers, founder-led brands. Where the editor workflow matters more than the rendering pipeline.

Drupal

Government, universities, healthcare. Complex content modeling, multi-site setups, granular role permissions, regulated industries.

Joomla

Association sites, member portals, community platforms. Built-in user management and access control are the core requirements.

WooCommerce

WordPress-native commerce, brands under five thousand orders a month, content-and-commerce mixed stores. Flexible and plugin-rich.

Shopify

DTC brands at scale, hands-off operations, headless Hydrogen storefronts. Where the platform handles tax, fraud, and PCI.

Wix

Brand sites under 30 pages, founder-led businesses, marketing teams that need a single drag-and-drop surface. We extend with Velo and harden SEO.

Squarespace

Creator and lifestyle brands, portfolios, restaurants, boutique commerce under a thousand SKUs. Theme customisation + CRM integration.

Webflow

Designer-led marketing sites, B2B SaaS landing-page estates, brands that ship campaigns weekly. CMS Collections + Logic flows + CRM plumbing.

Migration & replatforming

Move platforms without losing rankings.

Five replatforming paths we ship most often — planned around the redirect map first, the new stack second.

Wix or Squarespace → WordPress

Content extracted, modeled into custom post types, redirect map built from top-traffic pages first.

Drupal → Headless (Sanity + Next.js)

Branch-by-abstraction with both stacks running side-by-side. Cut-over with zero downtime, SEO preserved.

Legacy → Modern WordPress

PHP 8.2, block themes, Gutenberg. Side-by-side migration — not a destructive upgrade.

Wix or Squarespace → Webflow

Designer-led replatform for marketing teams that have outgrown builders. Content into CMS Collections, 301 map written first.

Medium or Substack → Ghost

Publishers moving onto owned infrastructure with paid memberships. Subscribers migrated, Stripe re-bound, theme rebuilt.
